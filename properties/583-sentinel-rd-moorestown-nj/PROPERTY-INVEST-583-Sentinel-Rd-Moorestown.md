However, **New Jersey's punitive property tax burden (~2.81% effective) crushes rental cash flow**, making this property a **poor fit for cash-flow-focused buy-and-hold** and a **non-starter for BRRRR** (no distressed inventory, ARV uplift insufficient to refinance out cash). **Investment Upside Score: 42 / 100** — Below average. Solid asset, wrong vehicle for most active investors. Best-suited for a 1031 parking play, a high-income owner-occupant, or a long-hold appreciation investor with patient capital.

## Strategy 2: BRRRR (Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat)

### Suitability Analysis: **NOT SUITABLE**

BRRRR requires three conditions, **none of which are met here**:

1. **Distressed/below-market acquisition** — Moorestown is a competitive seller's market (39 DOM vs. 55 national; 6.5% YoY appreciation). Distressed inventory is virtually non-existent. Most homes are turnkey or near-turnkey.

2. **Forced appreciation gap large enough to refi out 75-80% of all-in cost** — In Moorestown, a $700K dated home + $80K rehab = $780K all-in. ARV maybe $850K-$895K. Refi at 75% = $637K-$671K, leaving $109K-$143K stuck. **The ARV uplift cannot recapture the rehab spend** because the entry price is already near top-of-market.

3. **Rent supports debt service post-refi** — Even if refi worked, rents of $4,200-$4,500/mo cannot service a $640K loan + $22K taxes + insurance + management. The buy-and-hold math (negative $2,790/mo) confirms this.

### Why Moorestown Kills BRRRR
- High land value, low building value spread (the dirt is the asset)
- Buyers in this price range want move-in-ready and pay a premium for it; they don't reward cosmetic flips with disproportionate ARV uplift
- Property tax of ~$22K/yr crushes any rental DSCR

### Feasibility Score: **5/100**

**Verdict:** **Do not pursue.** BRRRR is a Cleveland/Indianapolis/Memphis strategy — not a NJ luxury-suburb strategy.

## Top 3 Risks

### 1. **NJ Property Tax Escalation (HIGHEST IMPACT)**
Moorestown property tax bills rose 2.4% in 2025 alone. The effective rate climbed from 2.76% (2024) to 2.81% (2025). On a $795K basis, every 0.1% increase = $795/yr in additional tax. **A 0.3% rate increase over 5 years adds ~$12K/yr to expenses, fully wiping out NOI.** SALT cap further limits write-off relief at the federal level.

### 2. **Interest Rate Sensitivity / Refinance Risk**
At 7% (used in this model), the property is already cash-flow negative. **At actual May 2026 investor rates of 7.5-8.0%**, monthly P&I rises to $4,167-$4,375, deepening losses by $200-$400/mo. Inability to refinance to a lower rate within 5-7 years would compound the negative carry. A 1031 exit would be forced.

### 3. **Market Downturn / Liquidity Risk**
Moorestown is a luxury suburb dependent on Philadelphia-area professional incomes. A regional recession (financial services, pharma layoffs) could push DOM from 39 to 90+ days and prices down 10-15%. **A flip caught mid-renovation in a downturn would face catastrophic losses** ($150K+ rehab capital trapped in a depreciating asset). For buy-and-hold, a 10% price drop on $795K = $79.5K equity wipeout.

### Honorable Mention Risks
- **SALT cap politics:** Federal cap could revert to $10K (was raised to $40K under 2025 OBBBA), reducing tax-deduction value for high-earner investors
- **NJ "Mansion Tax":** 1% surcharge on sales over $1M — limits ARV ceiling
- **Tenant quality at this price point:** $4,200/mo tenants tend to be transient corporate relos; turnover risk
