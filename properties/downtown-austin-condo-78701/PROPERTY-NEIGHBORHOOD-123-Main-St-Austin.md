## 9. Top 3 Strengths

1. **Unmatched urban convenience.** Walk Score of 92 — the highest in Austin. A resident can reach the Capitol, Lady Bird Lake trail, Whole Foods, two Google towers, the Convention Center, and 49+ restaurants without a car. This drives durable rental demand and supports premium pricing.
2. **Concentrated high-income, high-education tenant/buyer pool.** Median household income of ~$162K (2x the national average) anchored by Google, Meta, Oracle, Indeed, and state government employment. Population grew dramatically through the COVID-era tech migration and continues at ~65K/yr metro-wide.
3. **Cars-not-required lifestyle + transit access.** Red Line MetroRail, MetroRapid, Project Connect investment, and the Butler bike trail give a real car-light option — a rarity in Texas and a significant differentiator for younger renters/buyers.

## 10. Top 3 Weaknesses

1. **Weak in-ZIP elementary school access.** Pease ES closed in 2020. Families with young children must rely on Mathews ES in 78703 (good but cross-zone) or private/charter alternatives. This caps the family-buyer pool and pushes most downtown 78701 demand toward DINKs, singles, and empty nesters.
2. **Soft 2026 housing market with downtown condo oversupply.** Austin home prices are down ~2.2% YoY and forecast flat-to-negative through mid-2026. The 12% surge in housing stock means downtown condos face active competition from new deliveries; near-term appreciation will lag suburban submarkets.
3. **Quality-of-life friction from 24/7 urban core.** Property crime risk, 6th Street nightlife noise/spillover, homelessness visibility, and rising extreme-heat days reduce daily livability vs adjacent neighborhoods (78703 Clarksville, 78704 South Congress) that capture much of the lifestyle without the friction.
